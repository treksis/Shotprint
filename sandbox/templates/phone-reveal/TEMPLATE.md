# Phone reveal: back from the moon

## The film
A 177 s flagship-smartphone launch film (2026), built as one quiet argument. It opens on a photo of the
Earth rising over the moon. A pull-back shows the photo is on the screen of last year's phone, floating
weightless in a spacecraft cabin. One low, calm narrator asks "Once you've been to the moon, where do you
go next?". The picture dips to black on "Back to work." and the music hits as the lights of a white
engineering lab switch on. From there the film walks through the new phone part by part in the second
person ("you start with the chip... you add..."): teardown flat-lays, glowing chip graphics, thermal and
vapor-chamber renders, durability rigs, lens and aperture macros, then the camera out in the world (cavers
in light shafts, surfers, a photographer at a rock pinnacle at night) with its pro camera app. It ends on an
exploded view under a three-part recap ("A chip... faster than ever. A battery... longer than ever. The most
intelligent pro camera system ever."). The parts fly together into the hero, which hangs still in a dark
studio while its finish swaps on the beat. The callback "This is what happens when you go to the moon, come
back, and ask, what's next?" lands on a plain product name on black, followed by silence.

Measured: 116 shots, average 1.53 s, median 1.13 s (38.9 cuts per minute); long holds only on the opener
(8.51 s), the hero (5.96 s) and the end card. Product and CG shots sit at brightness 10-55 out of 255, the
white lab and flat-lays at 130-200, day exteriors at 70-145, and night and cave shots at 4-35.

## Arc
1. **Cold open (0-11.5).** Moon photo with a 3-line caption, pull-back to the floating phone, dip to black
   on "Back to work.", music hit, lab lights on.
2. **The chip (11.5-37.5).** Flat-lay teardown, then the chip, the die and the new etched chip name.
3. **Heat and battery (37.5-62.3).** Thermal, vapor chamber, a hiker in a vast valley.
4. **Toughness and finish (62.3-72.3).** Test rigs and colour frames.
5. **Designing the camera (72.3-88.1).** Lens macros and aperture blades.
6. **The camera in the field (88.1-154.2).** Caves, surf and night sky; music climax at 146.35.
7. **Recap and reveal (154.2-177.4).** Exploded view under the three "than ever" claims, the hero's
   finish swaps, the name on black on the final chord.

## Rules that make it this film
- **One narrator, never seen.** A low male voice (pitch ~97 Hz), dry and close, about 3.4 words/s. He
  speaks in second person and in short parallel clauses. Nobody on screen speaks. Every claim is spoken;
  none is written.
- **Product as a jewel in the dark.** Macros on black with one key and glowing edges; very shallow
  focus; the product's dusky wine (#6b3a45) is the only saturated colour in most frames.
- **White lab vs black void vs wild outdoors.** The film alternates three worlds: shadowless white rooms
  and flat-lays (130-200), black product and CG (10-55), and big natural places seen small (70-145 by
  day, 4-35 at night).
- **Cut on the music.** The opener's pulse phrases (1.30 s) and the finale's eighth notes (0.32 s)
  drive the cuts. The hero's finish swaps are hard cuts every 8 frames. The final chord lands 0.09 s
  after the cut to the name.
- **Restraint in type.** One 3-line caption at the start and the product name on black at the end. Both
  use a plain neutral sans and simple fades or hard cuts.
- **Shown "shot on" proof.** The phone's own photos and app screens appear full screen, pillarboxed when
  they are 4:3 or 3:4.

## Building it
- Every kept shot has its `render` mode in template.json. Product macros, flat-lays and the exploded view
  are first + last frame takes from product plates (product_phone, product_parts, product_chip). The
  opener's photo, the caption, the night photo, the camera app and the end card are drawn in code from the
  ui_ sheets (set/ui.json gives their timings). The hero is four still plates (one per finish) cut at the
  measured times.
- set/score.wav is the music without narration. Lay set/score_30.wav or set/score_15.wav, then place
  each narration clip at its cut `start`. Never retime a clip. A new film records its own lines to the
  same windows (lines.json) and keeps the rhetoric: a two-part question, a three-word answer, three
  parallel "than ever" claims, and a callback that ends on the cut to the name.
- Cast are invented (char_surfer, char_hiker) and seen small or in action. No one speaks on camera.
- Slots: {PRODUCT}, {PRODUCT_NAME}, {PREVIOUS_PRODUCT_NAME}, {CHIP_NAME}, {MISSION_LINE}, {DATE}, legal
  lines (see `slots`). No other brand's names, logos or app copy appear anywhere.
